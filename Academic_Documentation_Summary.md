## Project 1: Patent-GDP Panel Data Analysis

**File**: `Patent_GDP_Analysis_Documentation.md`

**Research Question**: Does patent activity drive total factor productivity (TFP) growth in developed economies?

**Methodology**:
- **Data Sources**: OECD patent database and Penn World Table (PWT) v10.01
- **Sample**: 8 countries, 1980-2019 (360 observations)
- **Econometric Approach**: Fixed-effects panel regression with country and year fixed effects
- **Key Variables**: TFP growth (gA) and patent growth (gPA) with various lag structures

**Key Findings**:
1. Contemporaneous patent growth shows statistically significant but economically negligible relationship with TFP growth (coefficient: 0.0041)
2. Within R² values (0.001-0.003) indicate patent growth explains virtually none of within-country TFP variation
3. Results suggest patent quantity may not be a reliable indicator of innovation-driven productivity growth

**Theoretical Contributions**:
- Formal derivation of growth rate calculations using logarithmic differences
- Mathematical specification of fixed-effects model with clustering
- Economic interpretation of within R² in panel data context

## Project 2: Modern Monetary Theory Analysis

**File**: `Modern_Monetary_Theory_Documentation.md`

**Research Question**: What are the theoretical foundations, policy implications, and limitations of Modern Monetary Theory (MMT)?

**Methodology**:
- **Theoretical Framework**: Mathematical modeling of MMT propositions
- **Analysis Tools**: Sectoral balance identities, Phillips Curve analysis, dynamic stability analysis
- **Critical Assessment**: Evaluation of job guarantee mechanism and inflation constraint hypothesis

**Key Findings**:
1. MMT represents an idealized framework that oversimplifies economic interactions
2. Job guarantee programs are economically infeasible due to supply-side distortions
3. Inflation constraint approach ignores central bank independence and market reactions
4. MMT's domestic focus overlooks international constraints and exchange rate considerations

**Theoretical Contributions**:
- Formal mathematical derivation of sectoral balance identity
- Game-theoretic analysis of government-farmer interactions
- Dynamic stability analysis of MMT policy framework
- Critical evaluation of job guarantee mechanism through Phillips Curve analysis

## Project 3: Price Fixing Comparative Analysis

**File**: `Price_Fixing_Analysis_Documentation.md`

**Research Question**: How do different forms of price coordination—illegal cartels versus legal supply management—affect market efficiency and welfare?

**Methodology**:
- **Theoretical Framework**: Game theory, welfare economics, industrial organization
- **Analysis Tools**: Nash equilibrium analysis, deadweight loss calculations, rent-seeking models
- **Empirical Evidence**: Canadian bread cartel case study and dairy supply management analysis

**Key Findings**:
1. Supply management creates larger welfare losses than cartels ($2.6B vs $500M consumer surplus loss)
2. Legal price coordination reduces innovation more than illegal cartels due to institutional persistence
3. Dairy supply management represents a stable political equilibrium resistant to reform
4. Supply management generates greater rent-seeking incentives than cartel arrangements

**Theoretical Contributions**:
- Mathematical modeling of repeated game dynamics between government and farmers
- Formal derivation of deadweight loss calculations for different market structures
- Game-theoretic analysis of political equilibrium stability
- Welfare analysis comparing consumer and producer surplus across market structures
